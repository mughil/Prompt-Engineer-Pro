# Worked Example: A Vague Writing Prompt

## Original prompt
“Write about the best running shoes.”

## Assessment
Illustrative score: 42/100. The topic is understandable, but audience, use case, evidence expectations, and output format are missing. The score is a judgment aid, not a prediction of model performance.

## Standard
```text
Write a beginner-friendly overview of how to choose running shoes. Cover fit, comfort, running surface, and budget. Use clear headings and finish with a short checklist. Do not name a single shoe as universally best.
```

## Professional
```text
Act as a practical running-gear editor. Help beginner runners choose shoes based on their running surface, weekly mileage, fit, comfort, and budget. Explain the trade-offs among road, trail, and everyday training shoes. Use headings, a comparison table, and a short checklist. Avoid unsupported medical claims and explain that fit varies by person.
```

## Expert
```text
Create a beginner-friendly buying guide for running shoes.
Audience: new recreational runners.
Goal: help readers shortlist shoes based on their needs rather than declare one universal winner.
Cover: intended surface, fit and toe room, comfort, durability, budget, and when to seek specialist advice.
Output: brief introduction, decision steps, comparison table, common mistakes, and final checklist.
Evidence: distinguish general guidance from claims that need current product testing; do not invent prices, test results, or medical benefits.
Success criteria: actionable, balanced, easy to scan, and transparent about individual fit differences.
```

## Improvements made
- Added a beginner audience so vocabulary and depth are appropriate.
- Replaced “best” with selection criteria because shoe needs vary.
- Defined the structure to make the guide usable.
- Added an evidence boundary to reduce unsupported product or health claims.

Confidence: Medium, because no budget, country, or preferred running surface was provided.
